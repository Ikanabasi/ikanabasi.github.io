Agentic AI Professional Networking Assistant

Overview

The short version of what this project is and why I worked on it.

The idea is not to build a bot that sends connection requests or messages for the lawyer. The useful part is the research and organization around that work: finding people, checking why they may be relevant, remembering previous decisions and surfacing meaningful updates.

Before building the backend, I’m creating a click-through prototype so the lawyer can react to the workflow, the wording and the approval steps. That lets me validate the product before spending time on integrations.

The problem

What needed to be solved before thinking about tools or models.

What made it difficult

  • Professional networking research is repetitive and easy to lose track of.
  • A fully automated LinkedIn agent would remove too much human judgment from a relationship-driven task.
  • The system needs long-term memory so it does not keep recommending the same people or forget prior decisions.

What I built / designed

  • Defined the end-to-end workflow from preferences to discovery, review and relationship memory.
  • Designed approve, save, reject and “not now” decision states.
  • Designed weekly email briefs that link back to a dashboard.
  • Planned connection tracking and meaningful public-web update alerts.
  • Planned AI-assisted comment suggestions while keeping posting manual.

How it works

A simplified view of the system or workflow.

Lawyer preferences→n8n workflow→Exa research→OpenAI analysis→PostgreSQL memory→Streamlit review→Human action on LinkedIn

Results so far

What is finished, working or learned at the current stage.

  • The system boundary is defined: AI researches and recommends; the lawyer decides and acts.
  • The core screens and approval flow are being prototyped before implementation.
  • The planned stack separates orchestration, memory, deterministic logic and AI reasoning instead of forcing everything into one model call.
